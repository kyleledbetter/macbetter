# Materials

<Lead>Materials are translucent layers that blur what's behind them. They keep people oriented: a menu or toolbar reads as floating above the content, and the content's color still shows through.</Lead>

<Demo name="foundations/materials" wallpaper hideCode />

## The materials

A material is the `mb-material` class with a `data-material` thickness. It sets a translucent background, the shared blur and saturation filter, a bright rim along the top edge and a hairline border:

```tsx
<div className="mb-material" data-material="thick">…</div>
```

| `data-material` | Background | Use it for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ultrathin` | Elevated background at 38% | Controls over photos and video, where the content should stay visible |
| `thin` | 54% | Small floating controls |
| `regular` (default) | 70% | Menus, popovers, context menus |
| `thick` | 84% | Surfaces with lots of text, or over busy content |
| `chrome` | Page background at 76% | Toolbars, title bars and navigation bars |
| `sidebar` | Sidebar background at 78% | Sidebars and source lists |

Each value is also a token, `--mb-material-ultrathin` through `--mb-material-sidebar`, for components that compose their own surface. They pair with `--mb-material-filter` (a 28px blur at 180% saturation), `--mb-glass-rim` for the top-edge highlight and `--mb-shadow-menu` for elevation:

```css
.inspector-popover {
  background: var(--mb-material-regular);
  backdrop-filter: var(--mb-material-filter);
  box-shadow: var(--mb-glass-rim), var(--mb-shadow-menu);
}
```

Macbetter's menus, dialogs, alerts, bars, windows and sidebars use these tokens, so they follow the theme and the accessibility settings below.

## Choosing a thickness

Thicker materials are more legible and less contextual. Start from the surface's job:

- **Text-heavy surfaces get thicker materials.** A long menu or a popover with paragraphs needs `regular` or `thick`.
- **Small controls over media get thinner ones.** A play button over a video can be `ultrathin` because it has one glyph and a large hit area.
- **Chrome stays consistent.** Use `chrome` for every bar in a window, so toolbars and title bars read as one layer.
- **Busy backgrounds need more.** If text over a material is hard to read in a screenshot of your real content, go one step thicker instead of adding a shadow to the text.

## Glass

`mb-glass` is a clearer, lensing surface for floating controls such as tab bars and toolbar capsules. It has a brighter top edge, a soft inner glow and a lighter blur than the materials. The [Glass](/docs/components/glass) component page covers its API and variants. The `glass` variant of [Button](/docs/components/button) uses the same look.

## Don't stack materials

Never put one material on another. Blurring a surface that's already blurred produces a flat, muddy layer, and the contrast of text on it becomes unpredictable. Inside a material, use fills (`--mb-fill-tertiary`) or separators to group content, not a second material. If a popover opens from a toolbar, it floats over content beside the toolbar; it doesn't need to look like part of it.

## Reduced transparency and increased contrast

When someone turns on Reduce Transparency (`prefers-reduced-transparency: reduce`), every material token resolves to its opaque background and `--mb-material-filter` becomes `none`. Increase Contrast (`prefers-contrast: more`) makes the materials opaque too, and strengthens the glass border. `mb-glass` becomes opaque under either setting.

This happens in the tokens, so a component built on them needs no extra CSS. If you write your own translucent surface, build it from `--mb-material-*` and `--mb-material-filter` rather than raw `rgba()` and `blur()`, or it won't respond.
