# Color

<Lead>Use semantic tokens, not raw colors. A token like `--mb-label-secondary` says what a color is for, and it stays right in light and dark, in every theme.</Lead>

## The Ember palette

The default theme, Ember, pairs warm oranges with deep greens. Orange carries action; Racing Green is the stage. The ratio on each swatch is its best contrast against white or Racing Green.

Rajah and Stromboli belong to the brand but aren't used by any token. Use them in illustration and marketing, not in controls.

## Semantic tokens

These swatches read the live CSS variables, so they follow the theme and scheme you pick in the header. Use the preview's sun and moon button to see the other scheme.

### Labels

Four levels of text, from `--mb-label` down to `--mb-label-quaternary`. They're all `--mb-ink` at decreasing opacity, so they sit correctly on any background, including materials.

- **Label** for primary text.
- **Secondary** for subtitles and supporting values.
- **Tertiary** for placeholders and hints.
- **Quaternary** for disabled text. It's deliberately below 4.5:1, so don't use it for anything a person needs to read.

`--mb-accent-text` is the accent adjusted to read as text. Use it for links and text-only buttons, never `--mb-accent` itself, which may be too light on a white background.

Classes `mb-label`, `mb-label-secondary`, `mb-label-tertiary` and `mb-label-accent` apply the most common ones.

### Backgrounds

`--mb-bg` is the page. `--mb-bg-secondary` and `--mb-bg-tertiary` form the grouped hierarchy of iOS settings screens: a secondary page with tertiary groups on it. `--mb-bg-elevated` is for surfaces that float, `--mb-bg-sidebar` for source lists, and `--mb-bg-control` and `--mb-bg-field` for bezeled controls and text fields.

### Fills

Four translucent grays mixed from `--mb-tint`, from `--mb-fill` (strongest) to `--mb-fill-quaternary`. They tint whatever is behind them, so use them for shapes that sit on varying backgrounds: switch tracks, segmented control tracks, hover and pressed states.

### Separators

`--mb-separator` divides rows and `--mb-separator-strong` marks larger edges. Both are translucent; `--mb-separator-opaque` is the same color mixed onto the background, for when overlapping translucent lines would show darker spots. `--mb-control-border` is for the edges of controls, and is derived to hold 3:1 against the background. `--mb-hairline` is the 0.5px width they're usually drawn at.

### Accent

`--mb-accent` fills the one primary action in a view. `--mb-accent-hover` and `--mb-accent-pressed` are derived lighter and darker in OKLCH, `--mb-accent-soft` is a 16% tint of it, the strength tinted buttons use, and `--mb-focus-ring` and `--mb-selection` are translucent mixes for the focus halo and selected text. Text on the accent uses `--mb-on-accent`.

`--mb-destructive`, `--mb-success` and `--mb-warning` point at red, green and yellow.

### System palette

Twelve colors named as in the HIG, from `--mb-red` to `--mb-brown`. Use them for status, categories and charts. The Ember theme replaces orange, green and mint with brand colors; the System theme uses Apple's values.

## Using color

- **Reach for the role, not the hue.** Write `var(--mb-label-secondary)`, not a gray. Write `var(--mb-destructive)`, not `var(--mb-red)`, when you mean "this deletes something".
- **One accent per view.** The accent means "this is the action". If everything is orange, nothing is.
- **Never rely on color alone.** Pair status colors with an icon or text, so the meaning survives color blindness and grayscale.
- **Derive, don't hard-code.** For a shade that doesn't exist, mix from a token: `color-mix(in oklch, var(--mb-accent) 24%, transparent)` or `oklch(from var(--mb-accent) calc(l - 0.05) c h)`. It then follows the theme and scheme.

## Contrast

Macbetter's targets are WCAG's: text at 4.5:1 against its background, and control edges, focus rings and state fills at 3:1, in light and dark. Under `prefers-contrast: more`, secondary and tertiary labels, separators and control borders get stronger, and materials turn opaque.

On a white background, Trinidad as text reaches only 3.5:1, which is why the theme uses a darker orange, `#B84300`, for `--mb-accent-text` in light mode. Text on the orange accent is Racing Green, not white, for the same reason. [Accessibility](/docs/foundations/accessibility) has the ratios for every built-in theme.
