# Level Control

<Lead>Set a light's brightness, a shade's position, a fan's speed or a speaker's volume with one tall slider.</Lead>

<Demo name="home/level-control-brightness" platforms={["ios", "ipados", "macos"]} wallpaper />

## When to use it

Use a level control in an accessory's control sheet for any characteristic that's a percentage: brightness, target position, rotation speed or volume. HomeKit stores all four as a percentage of the maximum, from 0 to 100.

- Put the minimum at the bottom and the maximum at the top, as the HIG asks of vertical sliders. A shade at 0 is closed and lets in the least light; at 100 it's open.
- Name it for the accessory and what it sets, like "Pendant lights brightness". In the interface, say "brightness" or "speed", never "characteristic".
- Show the value as words people understand: "80%", "40% open", "Medium".
- For a speaker that's a HomeKit accessory, a level control sets its volume characteristic. For audio your own app plays on iOS, use the system's volume view instead, as the HIG asks.

## Usage

```tsx
import { LevelControl, LevelControlValue } from "macbetter/level-control"
```

### Brightness

`tone="light"` fills with a warm white, and the icon at the bottom turns dark where the fill covers it. Put `LevelControlValue` inside to show the value above the track.

<Demo name="home/level-control-brightness" platforms={["ios", "ipados", "macos"]} wallpaper />

### Shades and blinds

`tone="neutral"` fills with white, the look for anything that isn't a light. A `format` function turns the percentage into words.

<Demo name="home/level-control-shades" platforms={["ios", "ipados", "macos"]} wallpaper />

### A fan

A fan is a level control with `steps`, for its discrete speeds, and a segmented control or switch for its direction. There's no separate fan component: HomeKit's fan service is a rotation speed, a rotation direction and an on state, and each has a control already.

<Demo name="home/level-control-fan" platforms={["ios", "ipados", "macos"]} wallpaper />

### Horizontal

`orientation="horizontal"` lays it across a row, like a speaker's volume in a Mac popover. It fills from the leading edge, so it mirrors in right-to-left languages.

## Platform behavior

- **iOS and iPadOS.** The large control is 124 × 300 points with a 34-point continuous corner, the size of the Home app's brightness control. It swells slightly under your finger while you drag.
- **macOS.** 88 × 224 points, or 56 × 160 at `size="md"`, sized for a popover. There's no hover state: the whole track is the target.
- **Every platform.** There's no knob. Press anywhere on the track to jump to that level, or drag the fill's edge; the fill follows the pointer one to one. The empty track is dark in both color schemes so a white fill holds 3:1 against it.

## Accessibility

- It's ui-sdk's Slider. The thumb is a native range input, invisible and stretched across the fill's edge, so screen readers on phones adjust it with their own gestures.
- Arrow keys step it, Page Up and Page Down move ten steps, Home and End go to the ends. Focus shows the accent ring around the whole track.
- `label` names it. `format` sets what screen readers hear, as well as what `LevelControlValue` shows.
- The icon is decorative and hidden from assistive tech.

### LevelControlValue

ui-sdk's `SliderValue` with macbetter's text style. Place it inside `LevelControl`.

### Data attributes

| Attribute | On | Values |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `data-tone` | Root | `light`, `accent`, `neutral` |
| `data-size` | Root | `md`, `lg` |
| `data-orientation` | Every part | `vertical`, `horizontal` |
| `data-dragging` | Every part | Present while dragging |
| `data-disabled` | Every part | Present when disabled |
